PRIMARY EDITION USED FOR COMPARISON
The Book of the Goetia of Solomon the King, translated by S. L. MacGregor
Mathers, edited/published by Aleister Crowley, 1904.
Digitized first-edition plates and printed pages:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu
Original scan source credited by Commons:
https://www.100thmonkeypress.com/biblio/acrowley/books/goetia_1904/goetia_text.pdf

The seals are on separate plates. Spirit number and figure number differ:
Buer: spirit 10, figure 11 on scan 6, descriptive text printed page 15 (scan 29).
Asmoday: spirit 32, figure 37 on scan 26, text pages 20-21 (scans 34-35).
Stolas: spirit 36, figure 41 on scan 26, text page 22 (scan 36).
Plate pages:
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Book_of_the_Goetia_(Mathers-Crowley,_1904).djvu/6

EDITORIAL LIMITS
The symbol variants follow this chosen edition, not a claim of one universal
unchanged seal in every manuscript. The widely circulated Celephais Press
2003 electronic re-set has different page numbers and is not the 1904 scan.
The courts and combat mechanics are fictional. Historical seals identify
these three courts; they do not add new rituals, spells or player abilities.
